# clinique almost lipstick: what Black Honey, Pink Honey and Nude Honey actually look like, what $25 really buys, and how to earn cashback on top

Two things send most people to search for Clinique Almost Lipstick. The first is the tube. It's dark, almost black, and looks like something that would land on your lips as full-on berry. It doesn't. The second is the price tag: $25 for 1.9 grams of product that behaves more like a tinted balm than a lipstick. That's a fair thing to squint at before you commit.

Here's what the product actually is, what each of the three shades does, where the price is genuinely firm and where it isn't, and how the stacked-savings layer works if you'd rather not pay full freight for a balm.

## What Almost Lipstick is, in plain terms

Clinique describes it as not quite lipstick and not quite gloss. The texture is closer to a tinted lip balm you can layer. Coverage is sheer, the finish is shine, and the size is 0.06 oz / 1.9 g — a standard slim lipstick bullet.

The formula is where the "balm" claim gets support. Clinique states 95% of the ingredients are conditioning, and the ingredient list backs that up: castor seed oil, glyceryl triacetyl ricinoleate, cetyl ricinoleate, candelilla wax, carnauba wax, and tocopheryl acetate (vitamin E). It's allergy tested, 100% fragrance free, and free of fragrance, parabens and phthalates. If you react badly to scented lip products, that's the relevant detail here, not the shade name.

One practical point that doesn't show up in most product copy: there's no opaque pigment to fix. You don't need a mirror. Clinique leans on that in its own marketing, and it's accurate for a sheer formula — a crooked swipe is invisible.

## The three shades, and why Black Honey behaves the way it does

There are three shades in the line: Black Honey, Pink Honey, and Nude Honey. They aren't three versions of the same color.

**Black Honey** is the one everyone searches for. Clinique cites Circana data for U.S. prestige makeup sales in the 12 months ending December 2024 to call it the #1 lip color shade in the U.S., and internal Clinique data covering February 2025 to January 2026 for the claim that seven Black Honey lipsticks sell every minute globally. The reason it reads differently on different people is the pigment mix: blue, red and a little yellow in a creamy, sheer base. Your natural lip color does part of the work. That is also why the tube looks intimidating and the result doesn't match it.

**Pink Honey** is the softer entry point. A sheer pink that adds a rosy tint and shine without much color shift. If you already wear a pinkish lip balm and want a slightly more polished version, this is the direct swap.

**Nude Honey** is the newest of the three, and it's built on a mix of warm and cool pigments rather than one flat beige. Nudes are the hardest category to buy blind, because "nude" means something different on every undertone, so it's worth reading the shade description as "your lips, slightly better" rather than a specific color.

## What it actually wears like, according to the reviews on the product pages

The Clinique product page shows a 4.7 out of 5 rating based on 471 reviews, with 1,907 reviews in total on the listing and a note that 636 of those came from incentivized reviewers. That's worth knowing before you treat the rating as neutral.

The praise repeats the same themes: sheer enough for daily wear, hydrating feel, forgiving application, and a color that reads as "my lips but better" rather than obvious makeup. A reviewer on the Clinique listing with a fair complexion described it as everyday color that doesn't require the full lipstick-and-liner routine.

The complaints are consistent too, and specific:

- **It doesn't love heat.** Multiple reviews note it gathering at the corners of the mouth at the beach or an outdoor summer event.
- **It isn't long-wearing.** Several reviewers describe it as a morning lipstick, and one Bloomingdale's review summed it up as moisturizing with a color that isn't too bright, but "a little pricey for its duration."
- **Hydration is subjective.** Some reviewers wanted more of it and layer a balm underneath, which shortens wear time further.

None of that contradicts the product. It's a sheer balm-hybrid, so it behaves like one. If you want a single application to survive lunch, coffee and a meeting, you're looking at the wrong category.

## Quick answers to what people keep asking

**Is Black Honey flattering on everyone?** It's designed that way, and the pigment logic supports it — blue, red and yellow in a sheer base lets your own lip color show through. It's still a sheer berry, so it will read warmer or cooler depending on your undertone. The tube is not a preview.

**Is it a lipstick or a balm?** Functionally a balm with sheer pigment. Coverage is sheer, the finish is shine, and 95% of the ingredient list is conditioning. Judge it as a balm and it performs well; judge it as a long-wear lipstick and it won't.

**How long does it last?** Not long. Reviewers consistently describe it as a morning lipstick and often reapply. Layering a balm underneath shortens it further.

**Does buying through ShopBack change the price at Clinique?** No. You pay the same amount at checkout. The cashback is a separate rebate funded by the merchant's referral commission, and it lands in your account after confirmation.

**Is there a hidden subscription?** No. ShopBack is free with no tiers and no recurring charge. The only subscription in this article is Sephora's Auto-Replenish option, which is a retailer program, not a ShopBack one.
